Who it is for

  • Blind and low-vision people who manage their own medicines and food allergies: about 55,000 in Finland, ~45,000 of them aged 65+; 2.2 billion people worldwide have a vision impairment.
  • Older adults whose sight is fading, with look-alike boxes.
  • The home-care nurses, pharmacists and family who check for them today.

What exists today

  • Braille on the box gives the name (EU law): not the pharmacy dosing sticker, expiry or allergens.
  • Seeing AI (phone OCR) needs a free hand to aim blind; reads raw text with no med or allergy check.
  • Be My Eyes calls a volunteer (private meds) or describes scenes with no safety.ts or pack-code check.
  • Built-in "Hey Meta" describes a scene without a 0.8 dose gate, personal regimen, or dose log.

Why glasses, not a phone

The camera is already looking at what your hands are holding.

Hands

Both hands stay on the box, the cane, the glass of water. No third hand for a phone.

Aim

First-person view: no blind aiming. The stream lets HoldUp coach you: “turn it around… a bit closer”.

Audio

Open-ear speakers and mic on your face. Ears stay open to the room; answers stay close.

Display

On Ray-Ban Display, a big verdict card in the lens. Shape + word + colour, for people with some sight.

Beyond mirroring the phone

A phone app takes a snapshot and reads whatever text it finds. HoldUp watches a continuous first-person stream, talks you into a readable view, and then answers one personal question: is this the right one for me, now? It is built for Ray-Ban Meta (camera via the Device Access Toolkit; the bridge APK compiles and its tests pass) and Ray-Ban Display (HUD Web App + Neural Band). Today the full loop runs on the phone camera, with the glasses as a Bluetooth headset; the on-glasses run is logged in docs/HARDWARE_LOG.md.

The product

One loop: ask, coach, read, check, answer, log.

  1. 01

    Ask

    “Is this my evening pill?” By voice, typed text, the big button, or a Bluetooth play/pause tap.

  2. 02

    Coach

    Gemini Live watches ~1 frame/s and gives one physical cue at a time. Cue arrow on screen and HUD, plus an earcon and a vibration.

  3. 03

    Read

    verify_label: up to 2 sharpest frames from the last 1.5 s (often 1 on the glasses at ~1 fps), strict JSON schema, a confidence for every field, null when not legible.

  4. 04

    Check

    Deterministic rules against your on-device profile: medicines, strengths, schedule, allergies, avoid list, expiry, today's doses.

  5. 05

    Answer

    The checker writes the sentence; Live is instructed to say it as written (FI/SV: fixed openers, details translated). The verdict card on the phone and in the lens is deterministic.

  6. 06

    Log

    “I took it” saves the dose on the phone, so next time: “You already took it today.”

MatchYes, Amlodipine 5 mg, your evening pill. You haven't taken it today.
WarningThis is Ibuprofen 400 mg. It isn't on your medication list and your list says to avoid NSAIDs.
Not sureIt looks like Metformin, but I can't read the strength confidently. Turn the box so the side panel faces you, or ask a pharmacist.
Verdict wording comes from server/src/safety.ts templates; medicine names shortened here (the demo profile stores “amlodipine besilate”).4

Architecture

One server, three thin clients, one Gemini key (server-side).

Code: server/src (index, live, verify, speculate, safety, hub, ledger) · web/src · android-dat/ · shared/types.ts contract6

Gemini inside the loop

Realtime Live with tool calls, plus a strict, honest reader.

Live API, mid-session tools

  • Audio + video in, native audio out, transcripts, barge-in.
  • Tools: verify_label, show_cue (non-blocking, silent), log_dose.
  • Session resumption + context compression: drops keep context.
  • Hard rule: Live never states a name, dose, expiry or allergen from its own view; it calls the reader and is instructed to say the checker's sentence as written (not enforced; the card is deterministic).

Schema-constrained reading

  • responseJsonSchema: every field is {value, confidence}, null when not legible.
  • Never infers strength from a brand; never completes a half-read word.
  • Reads Finnish and Swedish packs; ingredient mapped to its English INN name.
  • Thinking low, 8 s timeout, retry, fallback chain.

Speculative verify

While you are still asking, the server already reads the sharpest new frame (one call in flight, ≥ 2 s apart). The tool call is answered from that cache, or joins the read in flight.

Trust and safety

No clear read, no dose. It doesn't keep your pictures.

Honest answers

  • Per-field confidence, shown on the card: “What I read, and how sure I am”.
  • A strength counts only at confidence ≥ 0.8. A “yes, it's yours” also needs a confident name (≥ 0.8) and a readable label. Otherwise: Not sure, plus how to turn the box.
  • Deterministic rules, no model (safety.ts, unit-tested): wrong strength, not on your list, avoid list by drug class (NSAIDs), allergen synonyms (arachis oil → peanut, whey → milk, Finnish, Swedish), expired, already taken today.
  • Regulation: a med-list check is likely EU MDR software (Class IIa, Rule 11). So the pilot runs as a supervised study with NKL and pharmacists before any CE path. Uncertain cases always go to a pharmacist.

Privacy by design

  • Consent first: three plain cards before the camera can run. Withdraw any time.
  • Camera only during a check: you start it, CAMERA ON badge and sound, auto-stop after 45 s without a label, paused when the app is in the background.
  • Profile lives on the phone (localStorage). It travels with a check and is held only in server memory.
  • No server storage: up to 8 frames in a memory ring, dropped when the session ends. No image, audio or transcript logs.
  • Bystanders: both models are told to ignore people and never describe faces. The glasses' capture LED is on while streaming.
  • One button deletes everything on the phone.

Liability and regulation

Decision support, not diagnosis.

What HoldUp does, and doesn't

  • Confirms a pack against your own list. It never recommends or changes a dose.
  • A strength counts only at confidence ≥ 0.8.
  • “None of your allergens” needs allergen confidence ≥ 0.95 and readability ≥ 0.8.
  • “Not sure” is the default when a rule isn't met.
  • Any disagreement or uncertainty: ask a pharmacist.

Regulatory stance

  • Likely EU MDR software: Rule 11, Class IIa.
  • So the pilot runs as a supervised study with Näkövammaisten liitto and pharmacists, before any CE marking.
  • The profile and data stay on the device.

Resilience

A degradation ladder that says what still works.

What breaks and what HoldUp does
What breaksWhat HoldUp does
Gemini Live error or quotaWalks a 3-model Live chain; if all fail, says so and switches to snapshot mode: HTTP verify of the sharpest frames, answer read out by the phone.
Gemini socket dropReconnects with the session-resumption handle, so the conversation context is kept.
Phone connection dropBackoff with jitter; after two failed tries, snapshot mode; back to live automatically when the socket returns.
No internet at allOffline mode: recognises your saved medicines by barcode on the device, and says plainly it can't read new labels.
Slow networkFrame rate and JPEG quality follow the measured round-trip time (1 → 0.5 → 0.33 fps); frames are skipped when the socket backs up.
Reader failsOne retry, then 2 fallback models; if still nothing: “Not sure”, never a made-up answer.
Glasses off or disconnectedAnnounces it and switches to the phone camera; low battery (≤ 15%) is spoken.
Permission deniedPlain-language, per-platform steps to fix it, plus photo and typed-question paths.
Audio route changes“Audio now through …”, and the microphone restarts.
All states are visible live in the in-app device panel (mode, RTT, Live state, model, frames, glasses, HUD, auto-stop timer).10

Evaluation · Gemini as a workforce

We measure what matters: confident wrong answers.

  1. 01 · Flash

    Specs

    Gemini writes ground-truth labels: Nordic medicine packs, look-alikes, food with allergen synonyms.

  2. 02 · Image

    Render

    Gemini image models render a first-person photo of a hand holding each item.

  3. 03 · Flash

    Check

    A separate call marks each field legible and boxes it; garbled renders are dropped.

  4. 04 · CPU

    Degrade

    Blur, glare, rotation, crop, low light, back of box, at 3 severities.

  5. 05 · Product

    Score

    The shipped prompt, schema and safety.ts run on every image and profile.

Path to adoption

Pilot with the people who already help, on shipping glasses.

1 · Näkövammaisten liitto

Finnish Federation of the Visually Impaired. Proposed 4-week home pilot with 20 members on their own medicines.

Measure: confident-wrong answers (target 0), time to verdict, “would use daily”. Proposed pilot; user outreach in progress.

2 · Pharmacies

The pharmacist adds a new prescription to the profile at pick-up (name, strength, schedule, barcode scan already in the app), and is the hand-off for every “Not sure”.

3 · Home-care nurses

Wellbeing services counties' home care sets up the profile and checks the dose log on visits; HoldUp covers the hours in between.

Why it's feasible

  • Built for shipping hardware. Blind users already use Ray-Ban Meta with Be My Eyes, in 21 markets.
  • Phone-only works today as a PWA: no install, any phone with a browser.

Business model (ideas)

  • Free for users. Paid by pharmacy chains as an adherence service.
  • Procured as an assistive aid by wellbeing services counties.
  • Bundled with glasses for accessibility programmes.
